# The marketing brain

Every marketing job reads this first: social posts, listing pieces, listing pages, emails to the sphere,
open-house and launch copy. It holds the strategy: who we talk to, what we say, when we say it, and why.
The jobs' own playbooks say how to make each piece. This file says what the piece is for.

It improves from what works. `evidence.md` holds what we've measured (Chris's own history, top posts, what
clients say); `library/` holds the outside ideas we learned from, in our own words; the weekly review proposes
changes here when the evidence shows something better. Chris approves every change.

---

## 1. Who we are, in one breath
Chris Ranch and The Ranch Real Estate Group: two generations, 40+ years of Ranch family real estate in the South
Puget Sound, Keller Williams Luxury. A listing agent first. What clients say about us, in their words:
"made selling **so easy**", "**honest and upfront**", "**answered every question**", "**always on time and responded
quickly**", "**patient**", "**proactive**", "**went above and beyond**", "**in my corner**", "a true **class act**".
(From our reviews, source/content/reviews.json.) Use their words, not adjectives we'd pick for ourselves.

## 2. The story every piece tells (the client is the hero, Chris is the guide)
Adapted from the StoryBrand idea, in our words:
- **The hero is the client** (the seller, the buyer, the neighbor scrolling past). Never the house alone, never us.
- **Their problem:** on the surface, selling or buying a home. Underneath, the worry: leaving money on the table,
  the chaos, being in the dark, picking the wrong agent. Name the worry gently; don't dramatize it.
- **The guide (Chris):** shows he understands (empathy) and that he's done this many times (authority: results,
  reviews, two generations). One line of each, never a brag.
- **The plan:** simple and visible: the launch (photos, film, page, first week), the updates, the road to closing.
- **The call to action:** one clear next step, low pressure (see §5).
- **Success:** what life looks like after: sold well, calm, moved on; the new house feels like home.
Client-facing words still speak as Chris ("I", "we"); the system is never mentioned.

## 2b. How we talk about ourselves (Chris, Nov 25, 2026)
The best agents are fiercely protective of their clients and still promote themselves, without ego. The client is
the hero of the story; Chris is the guide who gets them there, and it's fine for the guide to be excellent.
- **Proof, not praise.** Let results, specifics and clients' words do the bragging. Not "I'm the best negotiator";
  instead "They accepted an offer nearly $25,000 over asking" (with the clients' OK), or a review in their words.
- **Credit the client, show the work.** "Their home went under contract in five days. Here's the plan we used."
  The win is theirs; the method is ours.
- **Protective, not possessive.** Talk about what we guard: their price, their time, their privacy, their peace of
  mind. "I protect my sellers' bottom line" reads as advocacy, not ego.
- **Confident and specific about us when it serves them:** two generations, 40+ years of Ranch family real estate, KW Luxury, the
  launch system, how fast we respond. Stated once, plainly, as a reason they can relax.
- **Never:** comparing ourselves down to other agents, "#1" or "top" claims we can't source, humble-bragging,
  making a client's sale about us.

## 2c. Persuasion that respects people
We use what's proven to help people decide well, never anything meant to get around their judgment.
- **Yes:** using the client's own words back to them; naming feelings ("It sounds like timing is the big worry");
  helping them picture the outcome ("Just imagine…"); reframing a worry into a plan; honest proof; one clear next
  step; for long nurtures, questions that help them find their own reasons to move (the motivational-interviewing
  approach), with patience.
- **No:** hidden commands, fake urgency, fake scarcity, pressure scripts, anything we'd be uncomfortable explaining
  to the client. We're fiduciaries first: trust is the whole business.

## 4. When to say what (the listing story arc)
Each listing is a story told over weeks, not repeated "just listed" posts. Each moment has one job:
| Moment | The post's one job | Hook pattern |
|---|---|---|
| Coming soon | Curiosity and early access | "Before it's public…" / one detail, not the whole house |
| Just listed | The home's strongest true line | the most specific, surprising real feature |
| First week | Proof the plan works | a real number: visitors, showings, saves (only if strong and true) |
| Open house | An invitation, with one reason to come | the room or view you have to see in person |
| A room or feature | Depth: a story about one space | "Did you notice…" (talker style) |
| Under contract | Proof for future sellers | the timeline in dates; "under contract in five days" |
| Sold | The result and the client's experience | the outcome + the sellers' words (with their OK) |
| After | The neighbor and sphere ask | "Curious what yours could do?" |
Between listings: local life (This Week in Gig Harbor, town guides), market notes in plain words, behind-the-
scenes of how we work, client stories (with permission). Roughly: 4 parts give, 1 part ask.

## 5. What to say (calls to action and soft openers)
Short phrases that make yes easy, in Chris's voice. Adapted from Phil Jones's "magic words" idea, in our own words.
Use one per piece, never stacked:
- "I'm not sure if it's the right time for you, but if you're curious what your home could do, I'm happy to walk you through it."
- "Just imagine a fall evening out on this deck."
- "How would you feel if the first week did the heavy lifting?"
- "What do you know about how homes in [area] are selling this winter?" (then answer it)
- "If you ever want an honest opinion on your home's value, I'm always happy to help."
- "Most people I talk to are surprised by…" (only with a true, sourced fact)
- "When would be a good time for a quick walk-through?"
- "Send me a message and I'll show you exactly how we'd launch yours."
Never: "DM me NOW", "Don't miss out!!", "Call today!", fake urgency, fake scarcity.

## 6. What makes people stop, save and share
- **Specific beats superlative:** "Mount Rainier from the primary suite" beats "stunning views". (Ogilvy's lesson:
  facts sell; the more specific, the more believable.)
- **A hook in the first line** that works alone in the feed. Five to nine words.
- **One idea per slide;** carousels earn saves when slide 2 rewards the swipe.
- **Proof with real numbers** (days to contract, visitors) and real client words: honest social proof (Cialdini).
- **Useful > promotional:** posts people save are ones they can use (a market note, a checklist, a local guide).
- **Share-worthy = makes the sharer look good:** local pride, insider knowledge, a beautiful home in their town,
  a neighbor's good news. Ask "would someone send this to a friend?"
- **Faces and voice:** Chris on camera or in first person builds trust faster than a logo.
- **Consistency** of look per listing (the chosen look) so the feed reads as one brand.

## 7. Luxury taste guardrails
Understated, specific, confident, warm. Never pushy, never hype, no exclamation runs, no emoji strings, no ALL CAPS.
Let photos breathe. Fewer words, better words. Price talk only when Chris says so. Discretion about clients always.

## 8. Words that must never appear (compliance)
- **Fair Housing:** describe the property, never the people who should live there. No "perfect for families",
  "family-friendly neighborhood", "ideal for young professionals/empty nesters", "adults only", "no kids",
  "exclusive", "safe/crime-free neighborhood", religious or ethnic references, or anything about disability other
  than describing a feature ("single-level living", "no-step entry"). The copy checker flags these.
- **NWMLS and brokerage advertising:** firm name (Keller Williams Realty Puget Sound) on advertising, accurate
  facts, no AI-made images presented as the real property, AI video never in NWMLS (CLAUDE.md rule 8).
- **Our rules:** every fact verified, client-facing words never credit AI, only Chris's direct line, no client
  names or details without their OK.

## 9. Pages and answer engines (AEO)
Every consumer page should be the best answer on the internet to the questions people actually ask
("What's it like to live in Tehaleh?", "Who's a luxury listing agent in Gig Harbor?", "How long do homes take to
sell in Bonney Lake?"). See `source/playbooks/pages-aeo.md`. Design stays locked; words, FAQs and structured data improve.

## 10. How we know it's working
For every piece: reach, saves, shares, messages, leads, page visits from it. The weekly review compares hooks, calls
to action, moments and looks, and proposes what to do more of. A pattern needs at least 3 examples before it
changes this file. "What's working" lives in `evidence.md`.
