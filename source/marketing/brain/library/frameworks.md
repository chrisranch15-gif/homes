# Frameworks we learned from (our own summaries)

Short summaries in our own words, so the jobs know where an idea came from. Not quotes, not copies.

**StoryBrand (Donald Miller, *Building a StoryBrand*).** Customers don't want to hear your story; they want help
with theirs. Make the customer the hero with a problem, position the brand as the guide with empathy and authority,
give a simple plan, call them to act, and show what success and failure look like. Used in brain.md §2.

**Phil M. Jones, *Exactly What to Say*.** Small phrases change how people respond: openers that lower pressure
("I'm not sure if it's for you, but…"), invitations to picture an outcome ("Just imagine…"), questions that let
people decide for themselves. Used in brain.md §5, always rewritten in Chris's voice.

**David Ogilvy, *Ogilvy on Advertising*.** The headline does most of the work; specific facts persuade more than
adjectives; write to one person; never bore. Used in brain.md §6.

**Robert Cialdini, *Influence*.** People follow honest proof from others like them (social proof), trust real
authority, value what's genuinely scarce, and say yes to people they like. We use only true proof and real
scarcity, never manufactured urgency.

**Chris Voss, *Never Split the Difference*.** Name the other person's feelings ("It sounds like timing is the big
worry"), ask calibrated "how" and "what" questions, and let people feel in control. Useful for seller conversations
and follow-ups.

**Gary Keller, *The Millionaire Real Estate Agent*.** Stay in front of the sphere on a schedule (the 36-touch year,
the 8x8 for new contacts) with value, not pitches. Used for sphere and past-client marketing.

**Institute for Luxury Home Marketing / KW Luxury.** Luxury listings are presented with restraint: architectural
photography, story over specs, the lifestyle and the place, discretion for the seller.

**Fair Housing advertising (HUD guidance).** Describe the property and amenities, not the people who should live
there. Words about who belongs (family status, religion, national origin, disability, sex, race, color) are out.
"Walking distance" and descriptions of features are fine.
